The rules

Can You Legally Work at 15?

Yes. In most countries, 15 is a legal working age, with restrictions. There are limits on how many hours you can work, what time of day you can work, and which tasks you're allowed to do. The rules exist so work doesn't hurt your schooling, health, or safety.

US rules: Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA)

The federal law that governs teen work in the US is the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA). For non-farm jobs, it sets these limits for 14- and 15-year-olds:

RuleLimit
Hours on a school dayMax 3 hours
Hours in a school weekMax 18 hours
Hours on a non-school dayMax 8 hours
Hours in a non-school week (e.g., summer)Max 40 hours
Earliest start time7:00 a.m.
Latest finish time (school year)7:00 p.m.
Latest finish time (June 1 to Labor Day)9:00 p.m.
During school hoursNot allowed (except approved school programs)

Good to know

  • 1

    State laws can be stricter. When state and federal rules differ, the employer must follow whichever rule protects you more. Always check your state labor department website.

  • 2

    School work programs are an exception. Students in a Work Experience and Career Exploration Program (WECEP) may work more hours during school weeks, including some hours during the school day.

  • 3

    Farm work has separate rules. Agricultural jobs follow different age and hour limits than regular jobs.

  • 4

    Some work has no age limit at all, such as acting or performing, delivering newspapers, and working in a business fully owned by your parents (as long as it isn't hazardous).

Do you need a work permit at 15?

It depends on your state. Many US states require minors under 16 to get a work permit (also called working papers or an employment certificate) before starting a job. A few states don't require permits at all. The process usually looks like this:

  1. Get a job offer from an employer.

  2. Pick up the permit form from your school counselor's office or state labor department website.

  3. Fill it out with your employer and a parent or guardian.

  4. Return it to your school or the issuing office for approval.

Money

How Much Do 15-Year-Olds Get Paid?

Pay depends on where you live and what kind of work you do. Here's what to expect, plus a quick calculator to estimate your monthly earnings.

🏪 Employed jobs (restaurants, stores, pools)

In the US, employers must pay at least minimum wage. The federal minimum is $7.25/hour, but many states and cities set a much higher minimum, so check your local rate.

📉 Youth minimum wage

Federal law allows employers to pay workers under 20 as little as $4.25/hour for the first 90 days of employment. Not all employers use this, and some states don't allow it.

🏡 Self-employed jobs (babysitting, tutoring, lawn care)

You set your own rate. These often pay more per hour than entry-level jobs because you're offering a direct service.

Off-limits

Jobs 15-Year-Olds Cannot Do (US)

Federal law bans 14- and 15-year-olds from hazardous work. This is why many restaurants hire 15-year-olds for front-of-house roles (host, cashier, busser) rather than cooking roles.

  • Manufacturing, mining, or processing plants
  • Construction or demolition
  • Driving for work or helping on a motor vehicle
  • Power-driven machinery (meat slicers, bakery mixers, lawn mowers at a business)
  • Most commercial cooking equipment like open fryers or grills
  • Long periods in freezers or meat coolers
  • Loading or unloading trucks
  • Working at heights on ladders or scaffolding
  • Door-to-door sales in most situations
Step by step

How to Get a Job at 15

Employers don't expect experience at 15. They look for reliability, a positive attitude, and willingness to learn. Follow these eight steps.

  1. 01

    Decide what kind of job you want

    • What do I enjoy? People, kids, animals, outdoors, or tech?
    • What's practical? Can you walk, bike, take transit, or get a ride?
    • What's my goal? Fast money, skills, or college-application experience?
  2. 02

    Check your state rules & permit

    Know your hour limits and whether your state requires a permit. Showing an employer you already understand the paperwork makes you look prepared.

  3. 03

    Look in the right places

    • Walk in to local restaurants and stores and ask for the manager
    • Company career pages and job boards filtered for teen or entry-level roles
    • School counselors, community boards, and neighborhood social media groups
  4. 04

    Tell everyone you're looking

    Let parents, neighbors, teachers, coaches, and family friends know. Be specific: "I'm looking for babysitting or weekend retail work." Most first jobs come through someone you already know.

  5. 05

    Create a simple one-page resume

    Even with no work experience, you have plenty to include:

    • Education: School name and expected graduation year
    • Skills: Communication, teamwork, time management, computer basics
    • Activities: Sports, clubs, band, student council
    • Volunteer work: Food drives, church events, clean-ups
    • Informal work: Babysitting, pet sitting, yard work
    • Achievements: Honor roll, awards, CPR or first aid certification
  6. 06

    Line up references

    Ask 2–3 adults who know you well: a teacher, coach, club advisor, or volunteer supervisor. Avoid family members unless asked, and always get permission first.

  7. 07

    Prepare for the interview

    Practice answers using examples from school, sports, or volunteering:

    • Why do you want to work here?
    • What's your availability?
    • Tell me about a time you worked on a team.
    • How will you balance work and school?
  8. 08

    Make a professional impression

    • Use a simple email (like firstname.lastname@email.com)
    • Arrive 5–10 minutes early
    • Dress neatly
    • Make eye contact and smile
    • Respond to calls and messages quickly
    • Check your public social media profiles
    • Send a short thank-you message after the interview
Stay on track

How to Balance a Job and School at 15

A job should add to your life, not take over it.

🌱

Start small

Try 6–10 hours a week before taking on more.

📚

Put school first

If your grades drop, cut back your hours.

😴

Protect your sleep

Tired teens don't perform well at school or work.

🗓️

Use a planner

Write down shifts, tests, and deadlines in one place.

🐷

Save every paycheck

Even 20–30% of each paycheck adds up fast.

💬

Talk to your manager early

Ask ahead if you need time off for exams.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the easiest job to get at 15?

Babysitting, pet sitting, dog walking, and lawn care are usually the easiest because you can start with neighbors and family friends. For employer jobs, grocery bagging and fast-food crew roles are the most common entry points.

How many hours can a 15-year-old work in the US?

Up to 3 hours on a school day, 18 hours in a school week, 8 hours on a non-school day, and 40 hours in a non-school week. Work must be between 7 a.m. and 7 p.m., extended to 9 p.m. from June 1 through Labor Day. State laws may be stricter.

Does McDonald's hire 15-year-olds?

Many McDonald's locations hire at 15, but since most are franchise-owned, the minimum age varies by location and state. Ask your local restaurant directly.

Can a 15-year-old work at a restaurant?

Yes. Fifteen-year-olds commonly work as hosts, bussers, cashiers, and dishwashers. However, cooking with fryers or using power-driven equipment is generally restricted until 16 or older.

Can I get a job at 15 without experience?

Absolutely. Most employers hiring 15-year-olds expect no experience. Highlight school activities, volunteering, and informal jobs like babysitting on your resume to show you're responsible.

What are the best summer jobs for 15-year-olds?

Lifeguarding, camp counseling, amusement park work, ice cream shops, golf caddying, and lawn care are great summer options. During summer break, you can work up to 40 hours a week in the US.

Can a 15-year-old work online?

Yes, but many freelance and selling platforms require users to be 18. Tutoring, design work for local clients, and selling crafts with a parent-owned account are good starting points.

How can a 15-year-old make $500 a month?

Working about 10–12 hours a week at a part-time job, or combining regular babysitting clients, dog walking, and lawn care, can get you to $500 a month depending on your local pay rates.

Do 15-year-olds need a work permit?

In many US states, yes. Some states don't require one. In the UK, many local councils require a child employment permit. Check your state or local council rules before starting.

Your first job is closer than you think.

Fifteen is a great age to start working. Whether you choose a structured job at a grocery store or restaurant, or start your own babysitting or lawn care business, you'll earn money and build skills like responsibility, communication, and time management that help for years.
